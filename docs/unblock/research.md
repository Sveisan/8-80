# Unblock — sources, influences and boundaries

The scripts and articulations are original. No book text is quoted, no author's voice is imitated, and no call mentions a book or implies endorsement. The owner's private notes, conversations and journals must never enter a prompt, fixture, transcript, seed or product record.

## Influences

| Influence | What it gave the module | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paulo Coelho, *The Alchemist* (1988) — owner's reading and conversations | The arc: a long-held wish; the comfort of keeping a dream a dream; signs that point; the early luck and the hard stretch that follows; finding that much of the treasure was where you started | Themes only, paraphrased in our own words. No quotations, character names, plot, "Personal Legend", "omens", "Maktub", or the idea that the universe conspires on anyone's behalf. Circumstances are never explained spiritually |
| David Bayer, *A Changed Mind* podcast — already reviewed for Beliefs, see [Beliefs research](../beliefs/research.md) | Concrete experience before abstraction; caller-owned conclusions; leave room for old patterns to recur | Belief work stays in Beliefs. Unblock names a block and moves on to a step; it does not prepare decisions or collect evidence. No spiritual causation, no claim that mindset alone determines outcomes |
| The 8&80 framework (README, SCRIPT.md) | The 8-year-old as the source of the wish and aliveness; the 80-year-old as the regret test and gratitude | — |
| Owner's conversations with Claude about books, 2026 | The request: a module whose goal is "to see the light in your own life, and living it" | Those conversations live in claude.ai and were **not** available to this design session; nothing from them is reproduced here. Owner should read [articulations](articulations.md) against them and correct what's off |

## Research that supports a mechanism (with limits)

| Source | Relevant finding | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gilovich & Medvec (1995), "The experience of regret: What, when, and why", *Psychological Review* | Over the long term, people tend to regret things they did not do more than things they did | Supports using the 80-year-old's regret question as a prompt. Does not mean every wish should be pursued |
| Gollwitzer (1999), "Implementation intentions", *American Psychologist* | Specifying when and where you'll act increases follow-through compared with a goal alone | Supports asking for a small, concrete step with a time. No promised effect size for phone calls |
| Emmons & McCullough (2003), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* — counting blessings | Regularly noting good things was associated with improved wellbeing measures versus comparison conditions | Basis for the light ledger as a hypothesis. Later replications show smaller and variable effects; the module promises nothing about mood |
| Behavioural activation literature (e.g. Dimidjian et al., 2011, *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology*) | Doing small valued activities, rather than waiting to feel ready, is a useful route out of stuckness | That evidence is for treatment delivered by clinicians to people with depression. Unblock is not treatment and must not be described as such |

## Product hypotheses to test, not facts

- Five weeks is long enough to take real steps and short enough to finish.
- Three light calls a week is sustainable; daily would feel like a chore.
- Hearing one's own noticed moments read back in the closing call is meaningful rather than saccharine.
- Callers can distinguish a real limit from a prediction when asked plainly, without feeling argued with.
- A finite programme with a designed ending is preferable to open-ended calls for this question.

## Language direction

Plain, warm, unhurried. One short question at a time. The caller's words, never a nicer paraphrase. Room for "I don't know", for a wish that changes, for a step not taken, for stopping. No absolutes, no destiny, no manifestation vocabulary, no praise for showing up, no counting.
