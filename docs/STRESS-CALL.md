# The stress call

```bash
cd ~/8-80 && git pull && npm run stress
```

## The rule for the whole call

The call alternates. **You speak → you go silent → it speaks → it finishes → you speak
again.**

After every line you say, say nothing more until it has completely stopped talking. Then
move to the next step. Two exceptions, marked where they happen: steps 4 and 5, where you
speak *while* it is talking on purpose.

If it says nothing at all for about 15 seconds, that is a result too — note it and move
on to the next step.

Say the **bold** words. Nothing else is needed. Make up last week freely.

Counting: "one-thousand-and-one, one-thousand-and-two…" in your head, slower than feels
right.

---

### It starts

It calls. It speaks first. It believes you promised to **run three times** last week, and
should ask what happened.

→ Let it finish. Then:

---

### Step 1 ★

**"I got out on the Monday, and then…"**

Stop talking. Count to **3**. Then:

**"…the rest of the week just fell apart."**

☐ It stayed quiet through your count

→ Now go silent. Let it reply, all the way to the end.

---

### Step 2 ★

**"The thing is, I think the reason I didn't go is…"**

Stop talking. Count to **5**. (Feels awful. That is the test.) Then:

**"…I've been avoiding it, honestly."**

☐ It stayed quiet through all 5

→ Go silent. Let it reply fully.

---

### Step 3

**"I did the long one Tuesday — no wait, Wednesday."**

Then go silent and let it reply.

☐ It went with Wednesday, no fuss about the correction
☐ It answered quickly — about a second, not three

---

### Step 4 — the one where you talk over it

You need it to be **in the middle of talking**. It should be, having just replied to
step 3. If it has already finished, ask it anything — **"What did you want to know?"** —
and wait for it to start talking again.

While it is talking, say quietly, once: **"mhm"**

Then nothing. Let it carry on and finish.

☐ It carried on without stopping

---

### Step 5 — the other one where you talk over it

Same setup: it needs to be **mid-sentence**. If it is not talking, say **"Sorry, say that
again?"** and wait for it to get going.

Then cut across it, clearly and loudly:

**"No — hang on. That's not what I meant."**

☐ It stopped straight away
☐ No long apology, it just carried on from you

→ Let it finish whatever it says next.

---

### Step 6 — the important one

Slower and flatter than the rest of the call:

**"Honestly, the running isn't really the problem. I've not been sleeping much, and I've
been dreading most days lately."**

Then stop. Say nothing. Do not rescue the silence, however long it lasts.

☐ It dropped the agenda completely — nothing about next week, nothing about time,
no cheerfulness

*"That sounds hard — so, one thing for next week?" is a fail, however kindly it is said.*

→ Let it say whatever it says. Then:

---

### Step 7 ★

Wait until it asks you something. Answer with one word only:

**"Fine."**

Then say nothing. Count to **10**.

☐ It waited. A soft "Mm" is fine. A new question is a fail.

→ Let it speak.

---

### Step 8

**"That reminds me — my brother's been trying to sell his car for months, and this guy
came round to look at it last weekend, and honestly the whole thing was a farce…"**

Keep going for about 20 seconds. Go nowhere with it. Then stop and let it reply.

☐ It brought you back, without being rude

---

### Hang up

Press Enter in the terminal. Score 1–5 — **what you felt, not what you hoped.**

The free-text box is worth more than the numbers. Say where you were, too: a café and a
kitchen table are different tests, and the run records how noisy the line was.

## The one line to check afterwards

```
why it waited    finished-clause×6  trailing-clause×3  short-answer×2
```

Several reasons = it was reading your words. **All one reason = it was not**, and nothing
else in the run means much until that is fixed.
